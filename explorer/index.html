Game reviews follow the Zaar World Tour

The markers are not noise: 20 of 20 random “tag” matches were forum games, and game reviews peak in the World Tour months (late May to early July).

The text alone gives the community away

A TF-IDF + logistic-regression model guesses the reviewer's community from one review, with users held out across folds. Removing game words, jargon and names barely changes it.

Taste differs, in the direction the game dictates

Recipe tags most over-represented in each community's reviews (lift vs all active users; tags with at least 300 reviews). The game community leans to drinks and world regions, which is what World Tour rounds assign. Descriptive only, no null.

Not a cohort, but a cohort exists

The game community joined when the mainstream did. The early cohort did not: most of its users arrived before 2006. Join year = first review on the site; KS = distance between join-year distributions.

Ratings: a negative result

Game reviews look slightly more generous, but the gap disappears within the same user: players rate generously everywhere, the game itself does not inflate stars.

Methods in one breath

  1. Bipartite user–recipe network from Food.com reviews (Majumder et al. 2019): 226,570 users, 231,637 recipes, 1.13 M reviews.
  2. Active core by iterative filtering: users with ≥ 20 recipes, recipes with ≥ 10 reviewers → 3,477 users, 10,636 recipes.
  3. Projection to users (weight = recipes in common), disparity-filter backbone at α = 0.05 (Serrano et al. 2009).
  4. Louvain (Blondel et al. 2008), best of 10 seeds; null: degree-preserving rewiring of the bipartite network, 20 times.
  5. Game markers by regular expression, validated by reading matches; classifier with user-grouped 5-fold cross-validation.
